# Project Methodology: Global Supply Chain Risk & Disruption Analytics

## 1. Project Overview

This project analyses shipment data to investigate operational performance, shipment disruptions, and potential supply-chain risk factors.

The objective is to identify patterns that can help logistics and supply-chain professionals understand disruption risks and evaluate delivery performance.

## 2. Business Problem

Shipment disruptions and longer delivery times can affect customer service, transport costs, inventory availability, and overall supply-chain efficiency.

This project investigates the following questions:

- How do disruption patterns differ across transport modes?
- How do disruption rates vary by product category?
- How are weather conditions associated with shipment disruptions?
- How does geopolitical risk relate to disruption rates?
- How does carrier reliability relate to shipment disruptions?
- How does average lead time vary by transport mode and product category?
- Which routes have the highest average lead times?

## 3. Dataset

The dataset contains 5,000 shipment records covering the period 1 January 2024 to 31 December 2025.

The fields include shipment dates, origin and destination ports, transport modes, product categories, shipment distances, weights, fuel-price indices, geopolitical risk scores, weather conditions, carrier reliability scores, lead times, and disruption indicators.

The dataset was imported into SQL Server for data profiling, quality checks, and preparation for analysis.

## 4. Tools Used

- Microsoft SQL Server Management Studio (SSMS)
- SQL
- Microsoft Power BI
- DAX
- Data visualisation and exploratory data analysis

## 5. Data Preparation and Quality Checks

The original dataset was imported into a raw staging table before being prepared for analysis.

The preparation process included:

- Checking the total number of shipment records.
- Checking shipment identifier uniqueness.
- Reviewing date ranges and categorical values.
- Profiling numerical fields.
- Checking for missing or blank values.
- Investigating unusual values and validating numerical ranges.
- Converting fields into appropriate data types.
- Creating a cleaned analytical table for Power BI.

The final analytical table was checked to confirm that the expected 5,000 shipment records were available for analysis.

## 6. Analytical Approach

The analysis uses descriptive statistics and comparisons across shipment characteristics.

Disruption rates are compared across transport modes, product categories, weather conditions, geopolitical risk levels, and carrier reliability levels.

Lead-time analysis compares average delivery times across transport modes, product categories, and routes.

These comparisons help identify patterns that may warrant further operational investigation.

## 7. Power BI Dashboard

The dashboard is organised into three pages.

### Page 1: Overview

Provides a high-level view of shipment volume, disruptions, disruption rate, average lead time, and disruption trends over time.

### Page 2: Risk Analysis

Examines disruption rates in relation to transport mode, weather conditions, geopolitical risk levels, and carrier reliability.

### Page 3: Logistics Performance

Compares average lead times by transport mode and product category and highlights routes with longer average lead times.

## 8. Business Value

The analysis can help logistics and supply-chain teams:

- Identify shipment categories and transport modes requiring further review.
- Monitor disruption trends over time.
- Investigate possible relationships between external risk factors and
  shipment disruptions.
- Compare delivery performance across routes and transport modes.
- Identify areas where more detailed operational analysis may be valuable.

The findings can support evidence-based discussions about risk monitoring, carrier performance, and delivery efficiency.

## 9. Limitations

This project uses the fields and records available in the supplied dataset.

Observed relationships do not establish causation. For example, a higher disruption rate within a particular risk category does not prove that the risk factor alone caused those disruptions.

The results should therefore be interpreted as descriptive findings rather than definitive causal conclusions.

Further analysis using additional operational data would be required to validate potential causes and estimate financial impacts.

## 10. Project Outcome

The project demonstrates an end-to-end analytical workflow involving data profiling, data-quality checks, SQL-based data preparation, DAX measures, and Power BI dashboard development.

It applies data analytics to practical questions involving supply-chain risk, shipment disruptions, and logistics performance.
